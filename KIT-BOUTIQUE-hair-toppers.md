```
WHAT IT IS

A hair topper is a small piece of real hair that clips onto the crown of your
head. It covers a widening part, thinning at the top, or visible scalp — while
blending completely with the hair you already have.

Unlike a wig, you keep your own hairline and your own length. Nobody sees a
transition, because there isn't one.


THE HAIR

100% Remy human hair, cuticle aligned. That last part matters more than
anything else on this page.

In non-Remy hair, the cuticles are collected in mixed directions and then
chemically stripped. It looks fine for two weeks, then it tangles, mats, and
sheds. That's why so many toppers end up in a drawer.

Remy hair keeps every cuticle facing the same way. It moves naturally, it
washes without matting, and it lasts.

You can wash it, straighten it, curl it, and style it exactly like your own
hair.


THE BASE

A 6" x 6" mono base with a hand-tied parting area. The mono construction lets

HOW IT ATTACHES

Four pressure clips, positioned around the base. Part your hair where you

# 4. PAGE « HOW TO WEAR IT »

```
PUTTING IT ON

1. Brush your hair and part it where you want the topper to sit.
2. Backcomb lightly at the roots where each clip will go — this gives the
   clips something to grip.
3. Open the front clip, press it into your hair at the front of the parting,
   and close it.
4. Do the same with the two side clips, then the back clip.
5. Blend by brushing your own hair over the edges of the base.

Total time after the first couple of tries: under 60 seconds.


BLENDING TIPS

- If your own hair is much longer, a few soft layers around the face help the
  transition disappear.
- A light texturizing spray on the topper matches its texture to yours.
- Positioning the topper slightly further back than your natural part makes
  the front edge invisible.


CARING FOR IT

Wash every 15-20 wears, or when it feels heavy.
Use sulfate-free shampoo, cool water, and don't rub — squeeze gently.
Air dry on a flat surface or a wig stand. Never wring it.
Heat tools are fine up to 350°F / 180°C — use heat protectant.
Store flat or on a stand, not crumpled in a drawer.

With normal wear and this routine, expect 6-12 months.
```

# 6. FAQ

```
Is this real human hair?
Yes — 100% Remy human hair, cuticle aligned. You can wash it, straighten it,
curl it, and dye it darker if needed.

What's the difference between a topper and a wig?
A wig covers your entire head. A topper covers only the crown and blends with
your own hair — you keep your hairline and your length. If you still have
hair on the sides and back, a topper looks far more natural.

Will people be able to tell?
Not if the color matches and it's positioned correctly. That's exactly why we
send free swatches before you order.

How do I know if my shade is right?
Order the free color-match kit. Compare the swatches to your own hair in
daylight, not indoor lighting. It's the only reliable method.

What if the color still isn't right?
Email us within 60 days. We'll exchange it and cover the return shipping.

Will it damage my own hair?
The clips attach to hair, not scalp, and there's no glue or tape. Move the
clip positions slightly every few days so the same strands aren't always
carrying the weight — the same advice applies to any clip-in.

Can I sleep or shower in it?
We don't recommend it. Take it off at night — it's a 10-second job and the
topper lasts much longer.

Can I swim with it?
Chlorine and salt water are hard on any human hair, including your own. If
you do, rinse it with fresh water right after.

How long does it last?
6-12 months with normal wear and proper care.

Does it work on very thin hair?
Yes, as long as you have enough hair around the crown for the clips to grip.
If your thinning is extensive, message us with a photo and we'll tell you
honestly whether a topper is the right choice — sometimes it isn't.

How fast do you ship?
Within 24 hours on business days. Delivery to the US in [X-XX] business days.

What's your return policy?
60 days. Free color exchanges. Refunds processed within 48 hours of receiving
your return.
